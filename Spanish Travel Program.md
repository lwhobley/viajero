# Spanish for Real Travel

## Your target

By next summer, you should be able to handle restaurants, transportation, hotels, and casual conversations in Mexico, Costa Rica, and Spain. You should also understand the main idea of everyday Spanish conversations you hear in Texas.

The course prioritizes listening and speaking. Grammar appears in small, useful pieces so you can build new sentences instead of memorizing a script.

## Daily rhythm

Complete one 30-minute core lesson each day. On days when you want another session, use the optional 30-minute listening lab.

### Core lesson: 30 minutes

1. **Sound warm-up — 3 minutes:** Repeat yesterday's phrases aloud.
2. **Scene — 5 minutes:** Meet the day's language inside a realistic travel situation.
3. **Pattern — 5 minutes:** Learn one small grammar pattern.
4. **Speak — 7 minutes:** Answer prompts aloud without reading a script.
5. **Listen — 7 minutes:** Hear or read increasingly natural versions of the scene.
6. **Recall — 3 minutes:** Produce five useful lines from memory.

### Optional listening lab: 30 minutes

1. Listen to a short piece of natural Spanish once without pausing.
2. State the topic and any words you recognized.
3. Listen again and write the key details.
4. Check a transcript or explanation.
5. Shadow the speaker: repeat immediately after each phrase, copying rhythm.
6. Retell the message in simple Spanish.

## How progress works

Lessons use a repeating three-day cycle:

- **Day A: Acquire.** Learn language in a new travel scene.
- **Day B: Use.** Role-play the scene with changing details and surprises.
- **Day C: Hear.** Decode faster, more natural speech and regional variants.

Every seventh day is a travel mission that mixes the week's skills. Vocabulary that you hesitate on returns later. Material you use easily becomes part of future conversations.

## First four weeks

### Week 1: Getting what you need

- Day 1: Ordering at a restaurant
- Day 2: Changing and clarifying an order
- Day 3: Understanding a server at natural speed
- Day 4: Asking where things are and following basic directions
- Day 5: Using taxis, rideshares, buses, and trains
- Day 6: Understanding transportation announcements and questions
- Day 7: Mission — arrive in town, reach a restaurant, order, and pay

### Week 2: Hotels and problem-solving

- Checking in and confirming details
- Asking about breakfast, Wi-Fi, checkout, and local recommendations
- Reporting a problem politely
- Understanding common staff responses
- Numbers, times, dates, and prices in real speech
- Making and changing plans
- Mission — manage a two-night hotel stay with one unexpected problem

### Week 3: Meeting people

- Introducing yourself naturally
- Saying where you live, what you do, and why you are visiting
- Asking follow-up questions
- Talking about likes, food, places, and plans
- Keeping a conversation alive when you lack a word
- Understanding the main idea without catching every word
- Mission — have a five-minute conversation with a local

### Week 4: Independent travel

- Combining past, present, and near-future ideas
- Getting detailed directions
- Handling missed connections and changed plans
- Asking for help and dealing with minor emergencies
- Comparing options and making decisions
- Regional listening: Mexico, Costa Rica, and Spain
- Mission — complete a simulated day trip entirely in Spanish

## Longer progression

- **Months 2–3:** Expand everyday conversation, descriptions, preferences, past experiences, and future plans. Build comprehension of connected speech.
- **Months 4–6:** Hold longer unscripted conversations, tell short stories, solve travel problems, and follow the main ideas of native-speed audio.
- **Final phase before travel:** Rotate full travel days, regional listening, and live conversation practice. Repair weak spots through spaced repetition.

## Regional awareness

You will learn broadly understood Spanish first, then recognize common alternatives:

| Meaning | Mexico / Costa Rica | Spain |
|---|---|---|
| computer | computadora | ordenador |
| juice | jugo | zumo |
| bill/check | la cuenta | la cuenta |
| you all | ustedes | vosotros / vosotras |

Costa Rica commonly uses **usted** in friendly situations and also uses **vos**. Recognition matters first; you can communicate clearly with **tú** and **usted** while you develop regional flexibility.

## Milestones

- **After 4 weeks:** Complete predictable travel exchanges and give a short introduction.
- **After 3 months:** Sustain a 5–8 minute guided conversation and identify the topic and key details in clear everyday speech.
- **After 6 months:** Handle unexpected travel problems and sustain a 10–15 minute conversation with repair strategies.
- **Before travel:** Complete a 30-minute simulated travel day and understand the main idea of unfamiliar, natural conversation.

## Rules that keep the course interesting

- No vocabulary list longer than ten new items.
- Every lesson includes a decision, role-play, or information gap.
- Scenarios rotate among Mexico, Costa Rica, Spain, and Texas.
- Correct only errors that block meaning or match the lesson's target.
- End while the activity still feels manageable; recycle difficult language later.
